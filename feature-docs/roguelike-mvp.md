# Roguelike Mode: MVP Proposal

A proposed first playable build of the roguelike Run mode, for **playtesting rather than balance**. Every number here is a starting point taken from simulation, and each is marked as measured or estimated. **Nothing is implemented, and this is a draft for review** — section 8 lists the questions I most want corrected.

Built from `upgrades.txt` (including the 9/14 and 9/15 notes), the three research rounds (`upgrades-research.md`, `-2.md`, `-3.md`) and the plan in `research/sim-plan-3.md`.

It would ship as a **third mode** beside Run and Classic, leaving both as they are: about 20 scripts in `research/sims/` import the shipped rules, and their baselines shouldn't move.

**Decisions made on 2026-09-15 that shape this proposal:** the 40–79 start with decade unlocks drafted during the run; all three systems (upgrades, money and shop items, relics) in the first build; slightly generous difficulty so most testers reach round 10; relics as a free pick of 1 of 2 at each shop; and both playtest aids available to testers as toggles.

**Design rules, from the 9/15 notes:**
- Difficulty never reacts to draft choices. A good or lucky draft makes the run easier.
- Low early targets, a super-linear ramp, so late rounds test the build.
- Upgrades need not be individually balanced; a weak pick that becomes strong in combination is the point.
- A little more forgiving: no razor-thin margins, no need to plan like a solver.
- High decades are a challenge to offer, not a penalty to avoid.

## 1. Run shape

| Setting | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rounds | 10 | unchanged |
| Starting pools | 40–79, nine tiles each, no repeats | round 3's measured start |
| Targets | **130, 135, 155, 185, 230, 285, 355, 435, 525, 630** | round 3 P2's calibrated schedule, eased ~5% and rounded |
| Max energy | 200, carried across rounds | unchanged |
| Refund | 5 energy per leftover turn on a win | unchanged |
| Optimal bonus | min(6, ceil(high word / 2)) | unchanged |
| Draft | after every round won (1–9): pick 1 of 3, or decline | `upgrades.txt` |
| Shops | after rounds 3, 6 and 9 | `upgrades.txt` |

**Why these targets.** Round 3 calibrated 135 → 239 → 660 so an average player drafting well won 93% and a random draft won 59%. Easing every target by ~5% measured 98.5% ±0.5 for that same well-drafting player, so this table should land a well-drafting player near 97–98% and a random drafter around 65–75%. The second number is an estimate: only the well-drafting case was measured. The table is written out rather than generated by a formula, because the round-3 schedule produced fractional targets.

**Par hints would be off in this mode.** `RUN_SPEND_PAR` and `RUN_NET_PAR` are simulation output tied to today's constants, and are wrong at retuned targets.

## 2. Upgrades (the draft)

Values are round 3 P2a's solo measurements on this exact start: **change in mean rounds cleared** when granted after round 1, 600 runs each, about ±0.03. They rank picks; they are not a balance target.

**Offer slot 1 — the spine.** Always the lowest decade not yet unlocked:

| Upgrade | Value |
|---|---|
| Unlock the 10s | +3.71 |
| Unlock the 20s | +2.70 |
| Unlock the 30s | +1.82 |

This guarantees progression, and makes the question "spine or build piece?" instead of "was I offered a decade?". It also stops two unlocks appearing in the same draft, which matters because round 3 found them strongly redundant: holding the 10s and the 20s is worth far less than the sum of each alone (−2.03 rounds cleared in controlled runs).

**Slots 2 and 3 — the build pool,** drawn by weight:

| Upgrade | Value | Weight | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Squares (a × a) cost 0 energy | +1.06 | uncommon | Fires when both pools share a number |
| Pool A limited to 10–49 | +0.86 | uncommon | Strong later; anti-synergy with unlocking the 10s |
| +40% round-end refund | +0.73 | common | Some is lost to the energy cap early |
| +40 max energy | +0.63 | common | Should also grant 40 energy immediately, as the sims do |
| Guarantee a 10–19 value in each pool | +0.59 | uncommon | Only offered once the 10s are unlocked |
| Remove the highest decade (the 70s) | +0.51 | common | |
| Optimal bonus cap +4 | +0.40 | common | |
| Keep the smallest number into the next round | +0.31 | common | Weak alone here; better once a low decade is in |
| +1 reroll of the board's largest number per round | +0.12 | common | |
| Remove two ones digits (4 named pairs: 5 & 8, 0 & 9, 5 & 6, 7 & 8) | ≈ +0.1, estimated | common | Measured only on 10–99, where the best pairs were worth +10 to +12 win points |
| **Cursed:** unlock the 80s, +50% refund | curse −19 points; the bonus makes it a close call | rare, from round 4 | Round 3 P5b |
| **Cursed:** unlock the 90s, +50% refund | curse −22 points; same | rare, from round 4 | Round 3 P5b |

**Suggested cut despite measuring well:** "+1 number per pool" (+0.50). It adds a tenth tile and a tenth turn, and the board is a fixed 3×3 grid with "9 turns" written into the tutorial — probably not worth the layout work before a first playtest.

**Suggested deferral:** the weight-based odds upgrades ("10s ×1.5") and the distribution chart from the 9/14 notes. They measured well, but they need the chart to be legible, which is more UI than a first build needs.

## 3. Money

| Source | Rate |
|---|---|
| Round cleared | 5 |
| Leftover turn | 1 each |
| Optimal play | 2 each |
| Overshoot | 0.5 per point above target, rounded down |

That's roughly 40 per round for an average player, so about 120 by the first shop. Round 3 P4 measured that overshoot pays nothing at 0.1–0.25 per point and starts costing runs at 2, so **0.5 sits in the band where it's a real choice** — which is the point. The 9/15 notes call out that a real player weighs a money play against an energy play, a decision the simulated players could not make.

## 4. Shops (after rounds 3, 6, 9)

**Step 1 — pick 1 of 2 relics, free.** Passive, last the run, so every run ends with three.

| Relic | Evidence |
|---|---|
| +40% energy refund on round win | round 1: +24 win points |
| +40 max energy | +17 |
| Optimal bonus cap 6 → 10 | +13; every Optimal play still costs net energy |
| Perfect squares (a × a) cost 0 energy | +19 on 10–99; +1.06 rounds on this start |
| Products over 4000 score double | **The spicy one.** Round 3: a skilled player wins 99–100% with it, an average player 24%. |
| Factors of 70 or more cost 25% less | +6 to +8.5; pairs with the cursed decade upgrades |
| +50% money per round | unmeasured |

**Step 2 — buy from 3 random items.** Inventory holds 3, discardable at any time.

| Item | Price | Energy equivalent (round 1, on 10–99) |
|---|---|---|
| Digit swap: change one tile from XY to YX | 40 | 10.7 |
| Next play costs half energy | 40 | 8.7 |
| Next play costs no energy | 80 | 17.9 |
| Reroll one pool | 80 | 22.1 saved for a bad board, 6.0 on any board |
| Double points on the next play | 120 | 23.7 |
| Board refresh: new tiles, same score and energy | 120 | 26.1 on a bad board |

**Suggested deferral:** "revive a factor" (its turn-count rule needs care) and "shopping app" (a meta-economy item).

## 5. Rules worth keeping as they are

- **Optimal judged on raw products,** before any relic or item modifier, matching the simulation harness.
- **Running out of energy still loses at exactly 0,** even if a zero-cost move were available (for example with "squares cost 0"). The sims share this rule, so their numbers stay comparable.
- **Overspending still loses,** and refunds still cap at max energy.
- **Both playtest aids available in this mode.** Worth noting on the feedback form which were on: Easy mode removes most of the arithmetic, so difficulty reports from those runs aren't comparable to the rest.

## 6. What to watch in playtests

This build exists to answer what simulation can't:

1. Does the draft feel like a choice, or does the spine slot feel mandatory?
2. Do players notice the redundancy between two low unlocks?
3. Is overshoot-versus-energy a real decision in the moment?
4. Is "products over 4000 score double" exciting or a trap? Simulation says it separates skill more than anything else tested.
5. Do items get used or hoarded? Round 1 found rerolls and refreshes are only strong when saved for bad boards.
6. Is round 10 (630 after 525) a satisfying final exam or a wall?
7. Where do runs actually end?

**Tuning knobs, in the order to reach for them:** the target array, max energy (200 is untested above), the money rates, item prices, relic timing.

**Suggested way to get data back:** end each run with a summary the tester can copy — picks in order, relics, items bought and used, round reached, energy left at each round end, money earned, and which aids were on. No backend needed.

## 7. Known limits of the numbers above

- Solo upgrade values were measured one at a time, granted after round 1, against a value-table drafter. Real builds stack, and round 3 found several "synergies" that vanished once the rest of the draft was good.
- Item energy equivalents come from round 1 on the 10–99 pools, with a simpler single-round player. Treat them as relative, not absolute.
- Relic values likewise come from round 1 on 10–99, except the two measured on this start.
- The random-drafting win-rate estimate for this target table is extrapolated from a ±5% sensitivity run, not measured directly.
- No simulated player values money the way a human will, which is the main reason to playtest the economy rather than tune it further.

## 8. Open questions for review

1. The spine slot guarantees a decade unlock in every draft until all three are taken. Does that make progression feel automatic rather than earned?
2. Three relics per run, one at each shop: too many, too few?
3. The cursed offers only appear from round 4. Right timing, or should a curse be available early, when the run is still cheap?
4. Money rates are set so overshoot is about half of income. Is that the tension you want, or should clearing rounds pay more?
5. Item prices assume roughly 120 money at the first shop, so one medium item. Should the first shop feel richer?
6. Anything on the item or relic lists you want added back that I deferred, or removed that I included?
